Budget cutoffs count agents whose SDK logs report a pilot spending denial. Completion rates include those attempts; token savings must be read alongside success and cutoffs, since stopping early also reduces tokens.

Two feature pairs from one Jinja task family are a smoke pilot, not a held-out evaluation. Timeouts, tiny sample size, shared family and partial structured-file capture limit interpretation. Baseline and treatment keep messaging, tools, model and verifier constant.

The [CooperBench paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.13295) reports roughly 25% cooperative success for GPT-5 and Claude Sonnet 4.5 in its setting. Different models, task selection and budgets prevent direct subtraction from those published rates; our completion delta uses our own matched baseline.

This preliminary pilot exercises capture, bounded prompt delivery and scoring end
to end. The 21.2% lower token count is descriptive; early delegation, iteration
limits, spending limits and model variation can all affect it. A larger comparison
is needed to assess usefulness. Eight blocks were delivered, with observation ages
of 4s median and 34s maximum versus a 300s half-life.

One inspected failure: in pair 1/6, the trail-arm agent reverted its edit and
finished after deferring to its peer over messaging. Its fallback feature was
then absent from the merged code. This is an observed case, not evidence that
trails caused the failure.

Model-free local timings for 300 events across 20 paths: deposit p50/p95
0.644/1.125 ms; lookup 0.563/0.843 ms; database 49,152 bytes. These sequential
SQLite timings exclude HTTP, SDK and model latency and are not a scaling result.

Both pinned task pairs passed the combined-reference verifier and failed on
untouched source before paid runs. All 27 offline tests and Ruff checks passed.
Native trajectories were not retained for the first baseline attempt; its usage,
activity and verifier records remain. Subsequent attempts retain agent outputs.
